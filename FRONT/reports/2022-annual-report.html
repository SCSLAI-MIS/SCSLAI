2022 Annual President's Report

Supreme Court Savings and Loan Association, Inc.

Financial Highlights

• LOAN PRODUCTION

SCSLAI maintains positive liquidity ratio by having enough cash to sustain its operations despite the global pandemic. This financial stability enables the Association to provide more loan facilities to its members thus assisting them with their financial needs.

Despite the limited face-to-face transactions and health restrictions set during the first half of the year, we still grant credits to our members by laxing the requirements and allowing them to avail loans coordinated through emails. With this, we kept the operations and business moving by encouraging our staffs and employees to exert more efforts by providing quality customer service thus allowing loan production for the year 2022 reached Php709,059,500.00 which is above the 2021 loan release of Php679,056,900.00 or an increase of 4%.

The growth is evident in the number of borrowers as the volume of loans increased by 6% from 9,105 in 2021 to 9,675 in 2022.

• COMPLIANCE WITH REGULATORY AGENCIES

SCSLAI is a financial institution supervised and regulated by Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP). Its financial statements as well as the governance through the members of the Board of Trustees are examined every two (2) years to ensure full compliance with all the circulars and to promote a well-managed non-stock savings and loans association for the benefit of its members.

Based on the previous audit, the Association has to strengthen its audit and compliance functions. As a response to this concern, the members of the Board designated key officers who will fulfill the duties and responsibilities of the Compliance Officer (CO), Internal Auditor (IA) and Risk Officer (RO). With their help and coordination, the Association looks forward to much better audit results for the coming years.

• CORPORATE SOCIAL RESPONSIBILITY

SCSLAI is committed to deliver its core values of giving back. Since the financial performance for 2022 is good, the Association decided to give back to the community by conducting an outreach program for cancer-afflicted children from far-away provinces who are temporarily housed in Silungan ng Pag-asa. A decent meal and some supplies are more than enough to gain the smiles of the kids who are suffering from terminal illness. This is the Association's way to express social responsibility — that in spite of the global pandemic, we are able to deliver our goals to the members and respond to the call of humanity.

Looking Forward

SCSLAI managed to remain stable amidst the test of time and through the global pandemic. This was shown in the previous financial and operational highlights we have discussed. But SCSLAI will continue to carry out its duty to serve its members even better with the following on-going and upcoming projects.

• EXTENSION OFFICE

Due to the volume of documents and files for retention for a couple of years, the Board of Trustees decided to use the Office Building Reserve for the construction of an extension office in the New Building. This will be used as the retention area for all the loan documents as well as the office area for the Compliance Officer, Internal Auditor and Risk Officer.

This was funded by the Surplus Reserve with a total contract price of Php2.7 million. In this regard, may we give emphasis to giving thanks to our very own Chief Justice Alexander G. Gesmundo, Atty. Maria Carina Matammu-Cunanan, Deputy Clerk of Court and Chief Administrative Officer, to our Clerk of Court, Atty. Marife Lomibao-Cuevas and Court Administrator Raul Villanueva for their invaluable support to SCSLAI's endeavor.

• LOWER INTEREST RATE AND NEW LOAN PRODUCTS

For 2023, the Board of Trustees introduced a new loan facility which we call the Business Loan that will help the members either consolidate their loans or choose from options, with a maximum loanable amount of Php500,000.00 at twelve percent (12%) interest rate per annum payable within five (5) years.

The members of the Board of Trustees also reintroduced the Housing Loan with more improved facilities such as a minimum loanable amount of Php500,000.00, provided that this will be secured by the member's capital contribution (CAPCON). The Housing Loan is payable within five (5) years with an interest rate of ten percent (10%) per annum.

We also decided to lower the interest rates of some loan facilities to accommodate the needs of the members. The Petty Loan was previously approved at Php25,000.00 with 12% interest per annum, however, in February 2023, the Board increased the loanable amount to Php50,000.00 payable in one (1) year and with an interest rate of five percent (5%) per annum.

Another loan with a lower interest rate is the Subsistence Loan. We decided to increase the loanable amount from fifty thousand pesos (Php50,000.00) to one hundred thousand pesos (Php100,000.00). The interest rate was reduced to 12% per annum instead of 16% and payable for two (2) years.

The Association’s normal operating consists of compensation, provision for credit losses and administrative expenses.

Compensation and fringe benefits consists of the staff’s salaries and wages, staff benefits as well as government mandatories like SSS, Philhealth and Pag-ibig contributions. There is significant increase in compensation since we have hired additional workforce for the year. Two Accounting personnels were hired during the year to facilitate more efficient and timely generation of reports to comply with the BSP’s time frame to avoid incurrence of penalties.

Compensation and fringe benefits are the salaries and wages of the employees and officers of the Association, including the mandatory contributions to SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG. The increase in compensation and fringe benefits is due to the increase in salaries and wages of employees.

On the other hand, provision for credit losses is the expense the Association incurred from uncollectible loan accounts. This arise when loan receivables become delinquent with no ways to collect from the principal as well the co-makers. We are recognizing this as an expense and will write off after the BSP approval.

Administrative Expense is consisting of the assemblies and conferences, director’s fee, representation and transportation, depreciation expense, as well as repairs and maintenance, stationery and office supplies among others. You may have noticed increase in total expense in 2022 since in 2021 there are still restriction as to face-to-face transactions.

One of the causes of increase in the administrative expense is due to the implementation of BSP directives with regard to giving sufficient trainings and seminars for all the staffs and members of the Board, thus increasing the Assemblies and Conferences account. Professional fees of accredited lecturers and facilitator falls into this category.

Total Expenses for the year 2022 was tallied at Php18,507,300.00 which is lower than the Php34,170,906.00 expense of 2021. This was mostly due to the loan-loss provisioning mandated by Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas on their 2021 examination and was already booked in 2021. However, there is increase in the administrative expense is due to the implementation of BSP directives with regard to providing sufficient trainings and seminars for all the staffs and members of the Board as well as increase in salaries and wages of employees.

As a result, the Association was able to record a net income of Php229,702,032.00 in 2022, 23% higher than the recorded 2021 net income of 2021 amounting to Php177,794,261.00.

With this level of profitability, the Association was able to declare an annual dividend of 16% for the year 2022 amounting to Php229,588,119.07 after satisfying Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas reserve requirements such as withdrawable share reserves, office building reserve and defined benefit obligation reserve.

Many are wondering regarding the stoppage of the acceptance of capital/savings deposits. This is because of the loan buy-out program of GSIS through GFAL, thus the Association has to solve the liquidity issue by utilizing the remittance from GSIS in order to yield good dividend rate. This was also implemented as one of the strategic initiatives of the Association to strike balance between granting competitive dividend rate while maintaining low interest rates for our loan products.
